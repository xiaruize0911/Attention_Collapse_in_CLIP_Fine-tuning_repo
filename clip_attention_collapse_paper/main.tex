\title{Attention Heatmap Drift in Fine-Tuned CLIP:\\A Controlled Matched-Learning-Rate Comparison of Full Fine-Tuning and LoRA}
\author{Ruize Xia}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Downstream adaptation often improves in-domain accuracy for CLIP while degrading out-of-domain transfer, but the structural changes that accompany this trade-off remain incompletely characterized. We study attention heatmap drift in CLIP ViT-B/32 under a strictly controlled design that matches learning rate across adaptation methods, and we combine that core experiment with selected auxiliary analyses from the broader repository study. The completed matched-learning-rate matrix contains 80 runs spanning Full Fine-Tuning (Full FT) and LoRA r=8, two datasets (EuroSAT and Oxford-IIIT Pets), four shared learning rates ($10^{-6}$, $5{\times}10^{-6}$, $10^{-5}$, $5{\times}10^{-5}$), and five seeds. We measure CLS-to-patch attention entropy, effective receptive field (ERF@0.95), Gini concentration, head diversity, in-domain validation accuracy, and adapter-aware CIFAR-100 zero-shot accuracy. The controlled results support three main conclusions. First, learning rate is a first-order control variable for structural drift: on EuroSAT, Full FT transitions from entropy broadening at $10^{-6}$ ($+1.83\%$) to strong contraction at $5{\times}10^{-5}$ ($-3.99\%$), whereas LoRA remains entropy-positive across the full matched grid ($+0.68\%$ to $+1.50\%$). Second, LoRA preserves out-of-domain transfer substantially better than Full FT at matched learning rates: averaged across the EuroSAT grid, CIFAR-100 zero-shot accuracy is $45.13\%$ for LoRA versus $11.28\%$ for Full FT; on Pets, the corresponding averages are $58.01\%$ and $8.54\%$. Third, Pets reveals an important interaction: low-learning-rate LoRA underfits badly in-domain, so method-only averages can obscure the regime in which LoRA becomes competitive. The retained auxiliary analyses are directionally consistent with this controlled picture: rollout and patch-to-patch summaries favor LoRA over Full FT on EuroSAT, LoRA remains closer to the pretrained encoder under layerwise CKA, and backbone validation shows that structural drift is backbone-dependent rather than universal. Across both controlled datasets, runs with broader retained attention support also retain more CIFAR-100 performance (entropy-vs-zero-shot correlation $0.61$ on EuroSAT and $0.90$ on Pets). These findings support attention heatmap drift as a useful descriptive lens on CLIP adaptation, while arguing for a more precise framing than a universal ``attention collapse'' claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Contrastive vision--language pretraining has made CLIP a strong foundation for transfer learning across a wide range of visual tasks \citep{radford2021learning}. In practice, however, the choice of adaptation strategy affects more than in-domain accuracy. Full fine-tuning can improve task performance while substantially degrading out-of-domain behavior, whereas parameter-efficient methods such as LoRA often retain more of the pretrained model's transfer capability \citep{kumar2022finetuning,wortsman2022robust,hu2022lora}. What remains less clear is how these adaptation choices alter the internal attention structure of the visual encoder.

This question is especially relevant for Vision Transformers, whose information routing is mediated by self-attention across spatial tokens \citep{dosovitskiy2021image}. Although attention maps should not be treated as causal explanations of model predictions \citep{jain2019attention,wiegreffe2019attention}, they can still serve as descriptive measurements of how adaptation reshapes internal structure. In that spirit, we study \emph{attention heatmap drift}: the change in CLS-to-patch attention support induced by downstream optimization.

A key difficulty in prior comparisons between Full FT and LoRA is that the methods are often evaluated under different learning-rate conventions. That design makes it hard to disentangle method effects from optimization-scale effects. We therefore adopt a matched-learning-rate protocol in which Full FT and LoRA share the same learning-rate grid on the same datasets across the same seeds. This controlled design allows us to ask a narrower but more defensible question: under identical optimization scales, how do the two adaptation regimes differ in structural drift, in-domain performance, and zero-shot retention? We then interpret earlier repository results, such as rollout, patch-to-patch, regularization, and backbone analyses, as auxiliary validation rather than as primary evidence. This ordering keeps the paper's main claims tied to the completed controlled matrix while still preserving the useful additional evidence.

The contributions of this paper are fourfold:
\begin{itemize}
    \item an 80-run controlled matrix comparing Full FT and LoRA r=8 under a shared learning-rate grid on EuroSAT and Oxford-IIIT Pets;
    \item a run-level analysis of attention entropy, ERF, Gini concentration, head diversity, validation accuracy, and adapter-aware CIFAR-100 zero-shot accuracy;
    \item a heatmap-centered visualization of matched-learning-rate trends and per-layer drift patterns; and
    \item a submission-ready synthesis that combines the controlled matrix with retained auxiliary evidence from the broader repository analyses, including rollout, CKA, regularization, and backbone checks.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{CLIP Adaptation and Robust Fine-Tuning}
CLIP established large-scale image--text pretraining as a strong basis for transfer learning across diverse downstream tasks \citep{radford2021learning}. A substantial subsequent literature then examined what happens when zero-shot models are adapted rather than used directly. In particular, \citet{kumar2022finetuning} showed that full fine-tuning can distort pretrained features and underperform under distribution shift, while \citet{wortsman2022robust} argued that robust adaptation of zero-shot models requires more care than simply optimizing for in-domain accuracy. These studies motivate the central question of the present paper: if downstream adaptation changes transfer behavior, what measurable internal structural changes accompany that shift?

Our work differs from this prior line in emphasis. The studies above focus primarily on predictive robustness and representation quality at the output level. We instead examine a structural correlate inside the visual encoder, namely the drift of attention heatmaps relative to the pretrained baseline. The paper therefore complements, rather than replaces, robustness-oriented analyses of CLIP adaptation.

\subsection{Transformer Attention as a Structural Object of Study}
The visual backbone of CLIP follows the Vision Transformer formulation of \citet{dosovitskiy2021image}, in which token interactions are mediated by multi-head self-attention. That architecture makes it natural to study how adaptation changes information routing across depth. Prior work on attention flow, especially attention rollout \citep{abnar2020quantifying}, provides one concrete mechanism for summarizing layerwise attention patterns beyond any single matrix. This broader literature supports the idea that attention can be analyzed as a structural property of the model even when it is not interpreted as a complete explanation of prediction behavior.

At the same time, the interpretability status of attention remains contested. \citet{jain2019attention} argued that attention weights should not automatically be equated with explanations, whereas \citet{wiegreffe2019attention} showed that attention can still support informative analysis when its role is framed more carefully. We adopt that more conservative position. In this paper, attention heatmaps are treated as descriptive measurements of how downstream optimization reshapes the visual encoder, not as stand-alone causal explanations of model decisions.

\subsection{Parameter-Efficient Adaptation and Representational Preservation}
LoRA introduced a low-rank parameterization of downstream updates that greatly reduces the number of trainable parameters while preserving much of the base model \citep{hu2022lora}. Although LoRA was originally developed and evaluated in language modeling settings, the broader idea of parameter-efficient adaptation has clear relevance for vision--language models: if the update space is constrained, one may expect the adapted model to remain closer to the pretrained representation geometry. This intuition is especially relevant for CLIP, where preserving pretrained transfer behavior is often as important as maximizing in-domain accuracy.

The present paper builds on that motivation but adds a sharper experimental control than most method-level comparisons. Rather than assuming that any observed Full-FT-versus-LoRA gap is attributable to the adaptation method alone, we explicitly hold the learning-rate grid fixed. This distinction matters because optimization scale is itself capable of inducing substantial representational and structural drift.

\subsection{Position of the Present Study}
Taken together, the prior literature leaves an important gap. CLIP adaptation work shows that robustness can degrade after fine-tuning, transformer-analysis work provides tools for studying internal attention structure, and LoRA offers a mechanism for constraining adaptation. What is less well established is how these pieces fit together under a controlled comparison that disentangles adaptation method from learning rate.

The contribution of the present study is therefore not a claim that attention analysis in CLIP is entirely new. Rather, it is a controlled matched-learning-rate evaluation of attention heatmap drift in which method, learning rate, transfer retention, and layerwise structure are analyzed jointly. This positioning also explains our use of the broader repository results: heterogeneous follow-up analyses are retained as auxiliary validation, but the paper's primary claims are tied to the completed controlled matrix.

\section{Controlled Study Design}
\subsection{Model, Datasets, and Matrix}
All experiments use CLIP ViT-B/32 (`openai/clip-vit-base-patch32`) with eager attention to enable extraction of per-head attention maps. The controlled matrix spans two downstream datasets, EuroSAT \citep{helber2019eurosat} and Oxford-IIIT Pets \citep{parkhi2012cats}, and evaluates transfer retention on CIFAR-100 \citep{krizhevsky2009learning}. We compare two adaptation methods:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Full FT, which updates the full visual encoder and classifier head; and
    \item LoRA r=8, which adapts the visual attention stack through low-rank updates while retaining a much smaller trainable parameter set.
\end{itemize}

The matrix is fully crossed over four shared learning rates ($10^{-6}$, $5{\times}10^{-6}$, $10^{-5}$, $5{\times}10^{-5}$) and five seeds (7, 11, 19, 42, 123), yielding 80 completed runs: $2$ datasets $\times$ $2$ methods $\times$ $4$ learning rates $\times$ $5$ seeds.

\subsection{Structural Metrics}
For each run we compare the adapted model against the pretrained baseline on a fixed, class-balanced evaluation subset. The primary structural measurements are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CLS-to-patch entropy}, where higher values indicate broader attention support;
    \item \textbf{ERF@0.95}, the fraction of tokens needed to accumulate 95\% of attention mass;
    \item \textbf{Gini concentration}, where higher values indicate more concentrated attention; and
    \item \textbf{Head diversity}, based on pairwise cosine dissimilarity across heads.
\end{itemize}

We report each metric as the percent change from the pretrained baseline. We complement the structural measurements with best validation accuracy on the fine-tuning task and adapter-aware CIFAR-100 zero-shot accuracy. The latter is important because incorrect evaluation of LoRA adapters can otherwise overstate transfer preservation.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Matched-Learning-Rate Trends}
Figure~\ref{fig:lr-panels} summarizes the controlled matrix. Several patterns are immediately visible.

On EuroSAT, Full FT is highly sensitive to optimization scale. At the smallest learning rate, it mildly broadens attention ($+1.83\%$ entropy, $+2.42\%$ ERF on average across seeds), but as the learning rate increases it becomes progressively more contractive, reaching $-3.99\%$ entropy and $-8.25\%$ ERF at $5{\times}10^{-5}$. LoRA behaves differently: it remains entropy-positive across the full matched grid, with mean entropy shifts between $+0.68\%$ and $+1.50\%$. The in-domain accuracy gap is largest at the smallest learning rate and narrows substantially at larger values. By $5{\times}10^{-5}$, LoRA reaches $98.83\%$ mean validation accuracy, close to the best Full FT regime, while still preserving much higher CIFAR-100 performance.

Oxford-IIIT Pets presents a more nuanced picture. Full FT is contractive throughout the grid, with mean entropy shifts ranging from $-1.57\%$ to $-3.63\%$. LoRA is structurally milder, ranging from essentially neutral at $10^{-6}$ ($+0.01\%$) to moderately contractive at $5{\times}10^{-5}$ ($-0.75\%$). However, low-learning-rate LoRA underfits the task severely: at $10^{-6}$ it reaches only $19.13\%$ mean validation accuracy despite retaining near-baseline zero-shot transfer. This underfitting regime is precisely why method-only averages can be misleading on Pets. Once the learning rate is large enough to optimize the task effectively, LoRA becomes competitive in-domain while retaining far more of the pretrained transfer behavior than Full FT. The best Pets LoRA regime in the matched grid ($5{\times}10^{-5}$) attains $91.91\%$ mean validation accuracy, slightly above the best Pets Full FT regime ($91.20\%$ at $10^{-5}$), while preserving substantially higher CIFAR-100 accuracy ($51.91\%$ versus $5.01\%$).

Table~\ref{tab:aggregate} provides a compact summary over the full grid. On EuroSAT, LoRA is both structurally broader and substantially more transfer-preserving than Full FT, albeit with a modest average in-domain accuracy penalty driven largely by the smallest learning rate. On Pets, the method averages should be interpreted with caution because the $10^{-6}$ LoRA regime is dominated by underfitting. Even so, the aggregate comparison still makes the structural pattern clear: Full FT is markedly more contractive than LoRA.

\subsection{Transfer Retention and Structural Drift}
The completed controlled study also sharpens the relationship between structure and transfer. The pretrained CLIP baseline reaches $60.22\%$ zero-shot accuracy on CIFAR-100. Against that reference point, the mean transfer loss under Full FT is severe across both datasets: $11.28\%$ average CIFAR-100 accuracy over the EuroSAT grid and $8.54\%$ over the Pets grid. LoRA retains much more of the pretrained capability: $45.13\%$ on EuroSAT and $58.01\%$ on Pets.

Importantly, the run-level association between structural preservation and transfer preservation is stronger than the association between structural preservation and in-domain validation accuracy. The entropy-vs-CIFAR correlation is $0.61$ on EuroSAT and $0.90$ on Pets, whereas the entropy-vs-accuracy correlation is weak to moderate ($-0.14$ and $-0.43$, respectively). We interpret this pattern cautiously. It does not imply that broader attention support causes better zero-shot transfer, but it does indicate that attention heatmap drift is a useful descriptive proxy for how much of the pretrained representation is retained during adaptation.

\subsection{Layerwise Drift Patterns}
Figure~\ref{fig:layer-heatmaps} visualizes mean per-layer entropy drift over the matched grid. The layerwise view adds an important qualitative detail to the run-level averages. Structural contraction is not uniformly distributed across depth. Under high-learning-rate Full FT, the largest negative shifts accumulate in the later layers, especially on Pets. At $5{\times}10^{-5}$, the mean layer-12 entropy shift is $-20.29\%$ for Full FT and $-11.53\%$ for LoRA. EuroSAT shows the same directional tendency under high-learning-rate Full FT, albeit less dramatically.

LoRA does not eliminate drift, but it changes its profile. On EuroSAT, the LoRA heatmaps remain positive across all learning rates, with especially large broadening in the middle and later layers. On Pets, LoRA becomes mildly contractive as the learning rate increases, yet the contraction remains substantially smaller than under Full FT. This distinction supports a more precise interpretation of the adaptation effect: the dominant failure mode is not universal collapse under all optimization settings, but strong late-layer contraction under aggressive Full FT regimes.

\section{Auxiliary Validation from the Broader Experiment Suite}
The repository also contains earlier follow-up analyses that were not part of the strictly matched-learning-rate matrix. We do not treat those results as replacements for the controlled study, because they mix learning-rate conventions or evaluate additional settings outside the main grid. We nevertheless retain the most useful of them as secondary validation because they reinforce the paper's central interpretation and broaden its empirical scope.

\subsection{Structural Triangulation and Representational Fidelity}
The first set of retained results triangulates the controlled entropy findings with additional structural measurements. Table~\ref{tab:auxiliary-validation} shows that on EuroSAT, LoRA r=8 produces slightly higher rollout entropy, larger rollout ERF@0.95, lower rollout Gini, and higher patch-to-patch entropy than Full FT. These auxiliary metrics are consistent with the controlled matched-LR conclusion that LoRA is generally more structurally conservative than Full FT.

The same table also reports a low coefficient of variation across five balanced evaluation subsets, suggesting that the reported entropy measurements are not highly sensitive to the specific subset of images used for the structural analysis. That stability matters because it reduces the concern that the observed drift patterns are merely artifacts of evaluation-image selection.

Representational similarity provides another useful secondary lens. Table~\ref{tab:cka-entropy} shows that, in the earlier follow-up analysis, higher mean CKA to the pretrained encoder is associated with broader retained attention support ($r=0.585$ across 39 checkpoints). In practical terms, runs that remain closer to the pretrained representation geometry also tend to preserve broader attention support. This auxiliary result does not establish mechanism, but it is directionally consistent with the controlled entropy-versus-transfer correlations reported above.

\subsection{Backbone and Regularization Context}
The broader repository suite also contains two useful contextual checks. First, Table~\ref{tab:backbone-comparison} shows that backbone choice changes the magnitude and even the sign of structural drift on EuroSAT. On ViT-B/16, Full FT increases entropy where ViT-B/32 is nearly neutral or mildly contractive. This result strengthens the caution already implied by the controlled study: structural drift is not a monolithic phenomenon and should not be presented as though it were invariant across pretrained backbones.

Second, the retained regularization results show that explicit structural penalties can move Full FT toward broader attention support, but the evidence is not yet strong enough to support a central claim of reliable mitigation. Table~\ref{tab:regularization} indicates that APR and entropy-floor regularization can improve entropy relative to the unregularized Full FT baseline, and Table~\ref{tab:stats} shows that none of the tested regularizers survives family-wise correction in the current analysis. We therefore retain these results as informative follow-up experiments rather than as headline evidence.

\subsection{Retained Single-Configuration Results}
Some earlier single-configuration results remain useful when interpreted in the light of the controlled matrix. In the original EuroSAT rank ablation, LoRA r=4, r=8, and r=16 all produced positive entropy shifts ($+0.98\%$, $+0.58\%$, and $+0.44\%$, respectively) and materially higher adapter-aware CIFAR-100 zero-shot accuracy than the corresponding original Full FT baseline (40.51\%, 47.28\%, and 39.35\% versus 9.43\%). We do not elevate these values above the controlled matched-LR comparison, but they are consistent with the same broad interpretation: lower-rank adaptation tends to retain more of the pretrained structure and transfer behavior than unconstrained Full FT.

\section{Discussion}
The controlled results, together with the retained auxiliary analyses, support a narrower and more professional framing than a broad ``attention collapse'' headline. Three points appear robust.

First, learning rate is a first-order determinant of attention heatmap drift. The same adaptation method can appear expansive, neutral, or strongly contractive depending on optimization scale. Any claim about method-level structural behavior should therefore be stated jointly with learning rate rather than in isolation.

Second, LoRA is generally more conservative than Full FT under matched optimization scales, especially when transfer retention is part of the evaluation target. This conclusion is strongest on EuroSAT, where LoRA remains entropy-positive throughout the grid and preserves much more CIFAR-100 performance. On Pets, the comparison is more conditional because LoRA underfits at very small learning rates, but once that underfitting regime is excluded, LoRA remains both competitive in-domain and markedly more transfer-preserving.

Third, the completed results argue for treating attention heatmap drift as a descriptive structural measurement rather than a causal explanation. The positive association between broader retained attention support and better zero-shot retention is informative, and the retained CKA analysis is directionally consistent with that relationship, but neither result by itself establishes mechanism. Transfer degradation likely reflects a broader set of representational changes than any single attention metric can capture.

\subsection{Limitations}
This study has four main limitations. First, the controlled matrix is restricted to CLIP ViT-B/32; although we retain a useful ViT-B/16 auxiliary check, the main controlled claims should not be generalized automatically across backbones. Second, the transfer analysis centers on CIFAR-100 as the only main out-of-domain benchmark. Third, the paper compares Full FT only to LoRA r=8 in the controlled matrix; it does not claim exhaustive coverage of the parameter-efficient fine-tuning design space. Fourth, the auxiliary repository analyses are heterogeneous in design and are therefore used only as supporting evidence, not as substitutes for the controlled matrix.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented a completed 80-run controlled comparison of Full FT and LoRA for CLIP ViT-B/32 under matched learning rates, and it integrated the strongest auxiliary evidence from the broader repository study into that controlled narrative. The results show that structural drift in attention heatmaps is strongly modulated by optimization scale, that LoRA is usually more structurally conservative than Full FT under the same learning-rate budget, and that broader retained attention support is associated with better zero-shot transfer retention. The retained auxiliary analyses broaden that picture without displacing it: rollout, patch-to-patch, and CKA summaries are consistent with the controlled interpretation, regularization results suggest partial but not yet definitive mitigation, and backbone validation shows that structural drift is backbone-dependent rather than universal. We therefore recommend a restrained conclusion: attention heatmap drift is a useful empirical lens on CLIP adaptation, provided that method, learning rate, downstream task, and backbone are analyzed jointly.
