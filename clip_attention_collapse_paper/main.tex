\begin{abstract}
We study a narrower and more defensible question than the earlier draft of this project: which adaptation choices preserve pretrained CLIP structure, and does structural preservation predict retained zero-shot transfer? The revision pipeline standardizes the study around two OpenAI CLIP backbones (ViT-B/32 and ViT-B/16), three source-task adaptations (EuroSAT, Oxford-IIIT Pet, Stanford Cars), three transfer-only benchmarks (CIFAR-100, DTD, Caltech101), and two main adaptation methods (full fine-tuning and a fixed LoRA configuration targeting $\texttt{q\_proj}$ and $\texttt{v\_proj}$ with rank $8$). The primary outcomes are mean CLS-to-patch entropy shift, mean ERF@0.95 shift, mean layerwise CKA similarity to the pretrained model, and transfer retention. The revised repository writes all new artifacts to a separate \texttt{outputs/revision\_v2/} namespace, builds fixed split manifests once, evaluates LoRA through the same adapter-active inference path as full fine-tuning, and regenerates manuscript tables and figures only from the new outputs. This framing makes three claims testable without overreach: full fine-tuning produces larger structural drift than LoRA, learning rate is a major control variable for structural drift, and structural preservation is associated with retained zero-shot transfer.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
CLIP adaptation often improves in-domain accuracy while reducing transfer performance. The original repository already surfaced the right open problems: too much emphasis on exploratory regularization, too many loosely connected metrics, and too little replicated evidence across seeds, backbones, and transfer benchmarks. This revision therefore treats structural preservation as the central object of study and narrows the manuscript to the comparison that the codebase can support cleanly.

The revised paper asks a single question: \emph{which adaptation choices preserve pretrained CLIP structure, and does structural preservation predict retained transfer performance?} We use attention structure as a descriptive measurement rather than a causal explanation, and we shift inference from layer-level comparisons to run-level summaries. This makes the study easier to reproduce, statistically cleaner, and better aligned with the observed failure mode in the earlier draft: adaptation settings that drift farther from the pretrained model also tend to degrade transfer more severely.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Experimental Scope}
The new pipeline uses only OpenAI CLIP ViT-B backbones from the same pretraining family: \texttt{openai/clip-vit-base-patch32} as the primary study model and \texttt{openai/clip-vit-base-patch16} as the confirmation backbone. Source-task adaptation is limited to EuroSAT, Oxford-IIIT Pet, and Stanford Cars. Transfer-only evaluation is limited to CIFAR-100, DTD, and Caltech101. This removes the earlier confounds introduced by broader model mixing and by treating Flowers102 as central evidence despite unreliable behavior in the previous evaluation setup.

Adaptation methods are intentionally minimal. The pretrained model is included as an evaluation-only reference. Full fine-tuning uses a fixed reference learning rate of $10^{-5}$ in the main comparison, while LoRA uses a single preregistered configuration with target modules \texttt{q\_proj} and \texttt{v\_proj}, rank $8$, alpha $16$, and dropout $0.0$. A focused full fine-tuning learning-rate sweep on EuroSAT ViT-B/32 isolates optimization scale as a control variable. Regularization is retained only as an appendix-level entropy-floor check rather than as a main contribution.

\subsection{Data Protocol}
The repository now materializes fixed manifests under \texttt{outputs/revision\_v2/manifests/} and reuses them across seeds and backbones. EuroSAT uses one saved stratified $80/10/10$ split. Oxford-IIIT Pet uses the official \texttt{trainval/test} split, with a stratified $10\%$ validation carve-out from \texttt{trainval}. Stanford Cars uses the official \texttt{train/test} split, with a stratified $10\%$ validation carve-out from train. Transfer datasets are evaluation-only and never enter the adaptation path.

Preprocessing is checkpoint matched. Both backbones use their own \texttt{CLIPProcessor} instances, which makes evaluation deterministic and keeps comparisons focused on adaptation behavior rather than custom normalization or cropping choices. The training transform permits only minimal augmentation through horizontal flipping before processor execution.

\subsection{Primary Metrics}
Let $a$ denote the normalized CLS-to-patch attention distribution over the image tokens. The primary structural metrics are:
\begin{align}
H(a) &= -\sum_i a_i \log a_i, \\
\mathrm{ERF}_{0.95}(a) &= \frac{k^\star}{N}, \quad
k^\star = \min\left\{k : \sum_{j=1}^{k} a_{(j)} \geq 0.95 \right\},
\end{align}
where $a_{(j)}$ denotes the sorted attention weights and $N$ is the number of patch tokens. We complement these with mean layerwise CKA similarity to the pretrained encoder, computed from hidden states collected on the same evaluation batches.

The functional target is transfer retention:
\[
\mathrm{Retention} = \frac{1}{3}
\sum_{d \in \{\mathrm{CIFAR100}, \mathrm{DTD}, \mathrm{Caltech101}\}}
\frac{\mathrm{ZSAcc}_{\mathrm{adapted}, d}}{\mathrm{ZSAcc}_{\mathrm{pretrained}, d}}.
\]
Secondary metrics such as Gini concentration, head diversity, rollout statistics, and patch-to-patch entropy remain in the run summaries but are no longer the center of the manuscript.

\subsection{Run-Level Inference}
The unit of inference is the run, not the layer. The main ViT-B/32 comparisons use five seeds for each source dataset and method. The ViT-B/16 confirmation runs use three seeds. The learning-rate sweep uses three seeds per learning rate. Each trained run emits one summary row containing in-domain accuracy, primary structural shifts, secondary metrics, zero-shot results on the three transfer datasets, transfer retention, the manifest path, the checkpoint path, and a timestamp.

\section{Revision Stack}
The repository now contains a new \texttt{revision\_v2} package rather than extending the legacy \texttt{run\_all\_experiments.py} path. The new CLI exposes the following commands:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{prepare-data}
    \item \texttt{run-pilot}
    \item \texttt{run-main}
    \item \texttt{run-lr-sweep}
    \item \texttt{run-backbone-confirmation}
    \item \texttt{run-appendix}
    \item \texttt{analyze}
    \item \texttt{build-paper-assets}
\end{itemize}
All new outputs are written under \texttt{outputs/revision\_v2/}. The manuscript asset builder in \texttt{clip\_attention\_collapse\_paper/scripts/build\_assets.py} now depends only on revision outputs and no longer reads the legacy Flowers102, rank-sweep, or broad-regularization assets.

\section{Discussion}
This revision intentionally gives up breadth in exchange for sharper evidence. The paper no longer tries to establish a broad attention-collapse mechanism, nor does it ask the reader to accept weakly supported regularization claims. Instead, it concentrates on three claims that the revised experimental stack is designed to evaluate directly: full fine-tuning is more structurally disruptive than LoRA, learning rate is a major determinant of structural drift, and runs that preserve pretrained structure better also retain more zero-shot transfer.

Just as importantly, the repository changes make those claims auditable. Dataset splits are frozen, adapter-aware inference is shared across pretrained, full fine-tuned, and LoRA models, and all tables and figures are regenerated from the run-summary layer rather than from ad hoc experiment-specific JSON files. That separation between legacy outputs and revision outputs is part of the contribution of this implementation work: it makes the next experimental iteration easier to trust.
